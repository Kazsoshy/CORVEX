[Heading 1] 1. INTRODUCTION
[Normal] 1.1 Project Context
[Normal] 
[Normal] The increasing adoption of digital technologies has transformed how organizations manage field operations, inventory, and business processes across geographically distributed branches. Recent studies have shown that enterprise information systems, cloud-based platforms, and mobile technologies improve organizational performance by enabling timely information sharing, operational coordination, and data-driven decision-making across business functions [1]. Organizations operating across multiple branches require integrated enterprise information systems to centralize operational data, improve coordination among departments, and provide consistent access to information that supports efficient business operations [2], with employee use-context identified as one of the strongest predictors of successful enterprise mobile system adoption, reinforcing positive effects on field employee engagement and performance when such platforms are properly integrated into daily operations [3]. Likewise, digital transformation has become a key strategy for improving supply chain flexibility, operational responsiveness, and organizational resilience, particularly among small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) operating in dynamic business environments [4]; this is supported by evidence that mobile, QR-based inventory management applications allow real-time stock updates and secure multi-organization tracking through mobile devices [5], and that cyber-physical inventory control systems can extend this synchronization across multiple physical locations simultaneously, directly addressing the discrepancies that arise when inventory is tracked independently at each site [6]. Dashboard-based order management systems have likewise been shown to reduce operational delays by consolidating scattered transaction records into a single, centrally accessible interface for management review [7]. In the Philippines, however, many micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) still face challenges adopting integrated digital technologies because of fragmented business processes, limited technological capabilities, and reliance on manual operational procedures [8]. These challenges are significant considering that MSMEs account for 99.59% of all registered business establishments in the country, making digital transformation an important factor in improving operational efficiency and business competitiveness [9]. Similar operational conditions were observed at Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing, a multi-branch distribution enterprise operating in Davao City, General Santos City, and Davao Oriental. Based on interviews with the Operating Manager, field personnel, and warehouse staff, together with direct observations conducted by the researchers, the company continues to rely on manual field coordination, inventory monitoring, and branch information exchange, resulting in unplanned collector routes and no controlled, centralized visibility into customer payment history across branches. In comparable field-operation contexts, GIS-based route optimization has been shown to reduce travel distance by up to 9.40% and fuel costs by 11.6% when system-generated routes replace driver-dependent planning [10]. At the same time, risk-based access control frameworks have demonstrated the ability to restrict sensitive financial and customer data to authorized personnel based on contextual risk level rather than blanket access [11] — both illustrating measurable improvements a mapping- and access-controlled approach could offer BH Marketing's current unplanned routes and uncontrolled cross-branch payment visibility. These operational gaps collectively contribute to inconsistent field scheduling, delayed operational information, fragmented inventory and customer payment records across branches, and limited management visibility over field activities, highlighting the need for an integrated enterprise information system that supports map-based field operations, Inventory Centralization, centralized customer payment verification, and business activity monitoring.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Currency, Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing manages its collection operations, sales activities, inventory monitoring, and branch coordination through predominantly manual procedures. Based on interviews conducted with the Operating Manager and surveys and observations involving Branch Heads, collectors, sales agents, and warehouse personnel from the Davao City, General Santos City, and Davao Oriental branches, field collectors organize their daily collection schedules using receipts and payment due dates. In contrast, sales agents conduct canvassing activities within their assigned service territories by identifying prospective and existing customers through written addresses, manually prepared location sketches, and personal familiarity with the area. Collection and sales transactions are initially recorded using paper-based documents before being submitted to their respective branches for encoding. Branch Heads supervise day-to-day operations, monitor field personnel, and coordinate operational concerns with the Operating Manager. For inventory operations, warehouse personnel conduct physical stock counts, manually verify inventory records, and update stock information only after completing the counting process. Although the three branches operate under centralized management, each branch maintains inventory and customer payment records independently. Operational updates and inventory confirmations are communicated through phone calls, text messages, paper documents, and other manually transferred records. At the same time, company-wide customer payment verification requires manual coordination between Branch Heads, the Operating Manager, and the Owner before credit-related decisions can be made, as no centralized information system currently supports these operational processes.
[Normal] 
[Normal] The existing operational procedures create several challenges that affect the efficiency and coordination of daily business activities. During field operations, collectors manually determine the sequence of customer collection visits from the list of accounts scheduled for collection by evaluating multiple operational factors, including the customer's outstanding balance, the number of days the payment has been overdue, and the travel distance from the collector's current location, without a standardized method for objectively determining which accounts should be prioritized first. Because collectors assess these factors based on individual judgment rather than a standardized prioritization process, they may assign different priorities to the same set of accounts, resulting in inconsistent collection schedules, inefficient travel routes, increased transportation costs, and fewer customers served within a working day. Similarly, sales agents canvass within their assigned service territories using written customer addresses, manually prepared location sketches, and personal familiarity with the area to locate prospective and existing customers. Because customer locations and field activities are not organized through a centralized map-based visualization, both collectors and sales agents struggle to identify geographically adjacent customers, plan efficient travel routes, and organize field activities within their assigned service territories. Likewise, Branch Heads and the Operating Manager cannot readily visualize customer distribution, territory coverage, route assignments, or ongoing field activities for operational planning, workload distribution, and field supervision. Consequently, field operations become more difficult to coordinate, resulting in unnecessary travel, overlapping service areas, reduced field productivity, and limited managerial oversight. Moreover, collection and sales transactions are initially recorded on paper before being submitted to their respective branches for encoding, delaying the availability of operational information and increasing the risk of incomplete or inconsistent field records. In addition, collectors and sales agents often work in remote service areas where internet connectivity is intermittent or unavailable. As a result, personnel cannot always transmit transaction details immediately to the branch office, so they must temporarily record collection and sales transactions manually and encode them once connectivity becomes available. This delay increases the possibility of incomplete, duplicated, or inconsistent operational records across branches. Inventory management also remains inefficient because each branch maintains inventory records independently and updates them only after manual stock counting and transaction encoding. Consequently, authorized warehouse personnel from one branch cannot immediately access the inventory records maintained by other branches. The inventory information that needs to be shared across branches includes product availability, current stock quantities, stock movements, stock transfers, and inventory adjustments. These records must currently be verified through phone calls and manual coordination with the concerned branch before inventory-related decisions can be made. Because these inventory records are not immediately centralized and accessible across branches, stock information may become outdated, resulting in delayed inventory verification, inaccurate stock information, duplicate inventory records, and occasional overselling when another branch has already sold products. Customer payment information, however, requires controlled, not unrestricted, cross-branch access. Each branch maintains customer payment records, including outstanding balances, payment history, credit status, and collection records, separately. When a customer purchases on credit or applies for additional credit through another branch, the Branch Head, Operating Manager, and Owner need access to the customer’s consolidated payment and credit information across branches to verify existing obligations before approving or evaluating additional credit. Other users are not authorized to access the customer’s consolidated payment records. Under the existing process, this verification requires manual coordination with the branch holding the customer’s records, increasing processing time and the possibility of granting additional credit despite unsettled balances in another branch. Collectively, these operational gaps reduce field productivity, delay access to operational information, increase operational costs, and prevent management from immediately consolidating collection performance, sales activities, inventory status, customer payment information, customer credit line records, and synchronized branch operational records into a unified operational view. Without summarized, descriptive analytical insights, management struggles to monitor operational performance, identify branch-level issues, evaluate customer credit exposure, and make timely operational decisions across all branches.

[Normal] 1.2. Purpose and Description
[Normal] 
[Normal] This study aims to develop CORVEX: A Mapping-Integrated Field Operations and Inventory Centralization System for Multi-Branch Enterprises for Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing to improve field operations, collection account prioritization, Inventory Centralization, centralized customer payment verification, and operational coordination through a centralized enterprise information system. The system supports the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure by promoting digital transformation and strengthening information and communication technologies. Furthermore, the study aligns with the University of Mindanao’s research agenda on ICT-Enabled Systems and Services by developing an integrated information system that enhances organizational efficiency, operational productivity, and technology-driven service delivery.
[Normal] 
[Normal] CORVEX is a web-based and mobile-accessible information system developed for Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing to support map-based field operations, Inventory Centralization, customer payment verification, and business activity monitoring across its geographically distributed branches. The system provides six role-based user accounts: Owner, Operating Manager, Branch Head, Collector, Sales Agent, and Warehouse Personnel. The Owner and the Operating Manager have enterprise-wide access to monitor branch operations, review operational dashboards and reports, verify company-wide customer payment records, and oversee inventory, sales, collections, and operational performance. Branch Heads can monitor field personnel, supervise branch operations, verify branch inventory, review branch-level operational reports, and coordinate operational activities with the Operating Manager. Collectors receive daily collection assignments prioritized using the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method, record collection transactions, update customer payment information, generate digital receipts, and navigate assigned customer locations through the integrated mapping module. Sales Agents use the mapping module to visualize customer locations, organize canvassing activities within their assigned service territories, record sales transactions, and submit field activity reports, while Warehouse Personnel manage inventory records, update stock movements, monitor product availability, and synchronize inventory information across branches. Role-based access control ensures each user can access only the information and functions needed to perform their assigned responsibilities, while maintaining data security and operational integrity.

[Normal] 1.4. Scope and Limitations
[Normal] 
[Normal] This study covers the design and development of CORVEX: A Mapping-Integrated Field Operations and Inventory Centralization System for Multi-Branch Enterprises for Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing. The system supports daily collection activities, sales field activities, inventory centralization, customer payment verification, and field monitoring through four major functional components: (1) a map-based field operations module that visualizes customer locations, service territories, and travel routes while assisting collectors in prioritizing collection schedules using the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method and supporting sales agents in scheduling field activities within their designated locations; (2) an offline-capable field transaction module for recording collection and sales transactions; (3) an inventory centralization and customer payment verification module that maintains updated inventory information across branches while allowing only the Operating Manager and Owner to access consolidated customer payment records; and (4) a management dashboard that applies descriptive analytics to consolidate collection performance, sales activities, inventory status, customer payment information, branch performance, and operating expense reports to support managerial decision-making. The system accommodates five user roles: Operating Manager, Branch Head, Warehouse Personnel, Sales Agent, and Collector, each provided with role-based access according to their assigned responsibilities. The system is intended for deployment at BH Marketing's branches in Davao City, General Santos City, and Davao Oriental, and it manages records generated from the implementation period onward.
[Normal] 
[Normal] This study is limited to the requirements identified during the requirements-gathering phase at Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing. It does not include modules for accounting transactions, payroll processing, procurement management, taxation, or financial auditing. The mapping component is limited to visualizing customer locations, service territories, and travel routes generated from customer addresses through geocoding services and displayed using Leaflet.js and OpenStreetMap. It does not perform advanced Geographic Information System (GIS) spatial analysis, continuous GPS tracking, or live location monitoring; instead, it uses stored geographic coordinates for route visualization and field planning. The system is designed specifically for BH Marketing's workflow and organizational structure, and its applicability to other organizations may require modifications to accommodate different business processes, policies, and requirements. The system also assumes that authorized users provide accurate records, maintain updated customer addresses, and have internet connectivity for data synchronization; however, the field transaction module supports offline recording and synchronization once connectivity is restored. Finally, implementation is limited to open-source software technologies and the existing hardware infrastructure at BH Marketing to minimize costs; therefore, the system excludes commercial enterprise software, proprietary mapping services, and paid analytics platforms.

[Heading 1] 2. METHODS
[Normal] Figure 1.  Agile Model
[Normal] 
[Normal] Figure 1 illustrates the Agile software development methodology used to develop CORVEX: A Mapping-Integrated Field Operations and Inventory Centralization System for Multi-Branch Enterprises. The researchers selected Agile because the operational requirements of Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing were progressively refined during requirements gathering through interviews, surveys, and direct observations involving the Operating Manager, Branch Heads, Collectors, Sales Agents, and Warehouse Personnel. Rather than completing the system in a single development cycle, Agile enables the researchers to develop the system incrementally through iterative phases of planning, design, development, testing, deployment, and review. This allows stakeholders to evaluate and refine completed functionality continuously based on feedback. This iterative approach suits CORVEX because its major modules—including the map-based field operations module, customer prioritization module, offline field portal, Inventory Centralization module, centralized customer payment verification module, and operations dashboard—can be implemented, validated, and improved independently before moving to subsequent iterations. Recent studies have shown that Agile software development improves adaptability to changing requirements, promotes stakeholder collaboration, supports incremental software delivery, and enhances software quality through continuous feedback and iterative refinement [15], [16], [17], [18]. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Phase 1: Planning and Requirements Gathering
[Normal] During the Planning phase, the researchers identify the operational problems and system requirements of Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing through interviews with the Operating Manager, surveys involving Branch Heads, Collectors, Sales Agents, and Warehouse Personnel, and direct observations conducted across the Davao City, General Santos City, and Davao Oriental branches. The collected information will be analyzed to determine the system's functional and non-functional requirements. These include map-based field operations, customer prioritization for collection scheduling using the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method, Inventory Centralization, centralized customer payment verification, business activity monitoring, and offline field transaction recording. The phase also identifies project objectives, scope, user roles, and the required software and hardware resources.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Phase 2: System Design
[Normal] During the Design phase, the researchers prepare CORVEX's overall architecture based on the validated system requirements. This phase includes designing the PostgreSQL database, user interfaces, system workflow, role-based access control, and module interactions. The researchers also design the customer prioritization process by integrating the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method to rank collection accounts based on the customer’s amount due, days overdue, and travel distance from the collector’s current location. The map-based field operations module will also integrate Nominatim for address geocoding, Leaflet.js with OpenStreetMap for map visualization, and OpenRouteService for route generation. These tools support both collectors and sales agents during field operations.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Phase 3: Development
[Normal] During the Development phase, the researchers implement the system using React.js for the web-based user interface and PostgreSQL for database management. The map-based field operations module integrates Leaflet.js, OpenStreetMap, Nominatim, and OpenRouteService to visualize customer locations and territories and generate routes. The customer prioritization module implements the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method for prioritizing daily collection schedules. The team will develop additional functionalities incrementally until all required modules are complete, including the offline-capable Field Portal, Inventory Centralization, centralized customer payment verification, and the operations dashboard.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Phase 4: Testing
[Normal] During the Testing phase, the researchers conduct functional, integration, usability, performance, and User Acceptance Testing (UAT) to verify that the developed system meets the identified operational requirements. The SAW module can be validated by verifying the correctness of the generated customer ranking. In contrast, the map-based field operations module can be evaluated for customer location visualization and route generation accuracy. Inventory Centralization, customer payment verification, dashboard reporting, and offline data synchronization can likewise be tested to ensure all modules perform accurately, reliably, and consistently before deployment.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Phase 5: Deployment 
[Normal] During the Deployment phase, the team deployed the completed system for operational use at Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing. The team conducted user orientation and training sessions for the Operating Manager, Branch Heads, Warehouse Personnel, Collectors, and Sales Agents to familiarize them with the system’s features and operational procedures. The team also prepares technical documentation and user manuals to support system operation, maintenance, and future enhancements.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Phase 6: Review
[Normal] During the Review phase, the team collects user feedback to evaluate the effectiveness, usability, and performance of the implemented system. Stakeholder suggestions and observations are analyzed to identify enhancements for future development iterations. This phase ensures the system continuously evolves after deployment to meet operational needs while staying aligned with objectives to improve field operations, Inventory Centralization, customer payment verification, and management decision support across BH Marketing’s branches.

[Heading 2] 2.1 Data Gathering
[Normal] This study's participants were personnel from Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing across its Davao City, General Santos City, and Davao Oriental branches. Participants included the Operating Manager, Branch Heads, Collectors, Sales Agents, and Warehouse Personnel directly involved in the company's collection operations, sales activities, inventory management, and branch coordination. They were selected through purposive sampling because they directly participate in the organization's daily operational processes and can provide reliable information on existing workflows, operational challenges, and system requirements. Before conducting the study, the researchers obtained formal permission from BH Marketing management to administer the research instruments and conduct on-site observations.
[Normal] 
[Normal] To obtain comprehensive information on the company's existing operations, the researchers used multiple data-gathering techniques, including printed survey questionnaires, semi-structured interviews, and direct observation. The researchers administered printed survey questionnaires to the Branch Heads, Collectors, Sales Agents, and Warehouse Personnel to gather quantitative and qualitative data on their operational responsibilities and the challenges encountered during daily operations. The questionnaires were divided into several sections covering field collection activities, sales operations, inventory management, customer payment verification, branch coordination, cross-branch information sharing, and business activity monitoring. The researchers used a five-point Likert scale to measure respondents' level of agreement with existing operational practices. At the same time, open-ended questions allowed participants to describe operational issues and suggest improvements.
[Normal] 
[Normal] To further validate the survey findings, the researchers conducted a face-to-face semi-structured interview with the Operating Manager using a prepared interview guide. The interview focused on the organization's overall operational workflow, the responsibilities of each operational role, existing branch coordination procedures, Inventory Centralization practices, customer payment verification, operational reporting, and the challenges of managing geographically distributed branches. The interview also validated the operational requirements identified through the survey responses and clarified organizational policies that structured questionnaires alone could not capture.
[Normal] 
[Normal] In addition, the researchers conducted on-site observations across the company's branches to examine how collection activities, sales canvassing, inventory management, and branch coordination are executed in practice. Particular attention was given to how collectors prioritize customer visits, how sales agents conduct canvassing within assigned service territories, how warehouse personnel verify inventory, and how operational information is communicated among branches. These observations enabled the researchers to compare actual operational practices with the information gathered from the surveys and interviews, thereby strengthening the validity of the collected data.
[Normal] 
[Normal] The information from the surveys, interviews, and direct observations served as the basis for identifying the system's functional and non-functional requirements. The findings guided the design and development of the map-based field operations module, customer prioritization using the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method, Inventory Centralization, centralized customer payment verification, operations dashboard, and role-based access control to address the operational challenges identified at Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing.

[Heading 3] 2.2.4.	Feasibility Requirements 
[Heading 3] 2.2.4.1. 	Operational Feasibility
[Normal] The operational feasibility of CORVEX was evaluated through interviews with the Operating Manager, survey questionnaires administered to Collectors, Sales Agents, Warehouse Personnel, and Branch Heads, and direct observations conducted across the Davao City, General Santos City, and Davao Oriental branches of Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing. The data gathered revealed that the company's daily operations remain highly dependent on manual procedures for collection scheduling, sales canvassing, inventory monitoring, and cross-branch coordination. Collectors manually determine the sequence of customer visits based on payment information and personal judgment. At the same time, Sales Agents rely on written customer addresses, manually prepared location sketches, and familiarity with their assigned territories during canvassing activities. Likewise, they verify inventory availability and customer payment information through phone calls, text messages, and manual coordination between branches because they maintain operational records independently. These existing practices result in delayed operational information, fragmented inventory and customer payment records, inefficient field coordination, and limited management visibility over company-wide operations. The findings indicate that the organization can benefit from a centralized information system that supports map-based field operations, customer prioritization for collection activities, Inventory Centralization, centralized customer payment verification, and business activity monitoring across all branches.
[Normal] 
[Normal] The system is operationally feasible because it enhances BH Marketing's existing procedures without significantly changing established personnel responsibilities. Rather than replacing current workflows, CORVEX digitizes and integrates collection management, sales operations, Inventory Centralization, customer payment verification, and business activity monitoring into a centralized platform. The system supports collectors by implementing the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method to prioritize daily collection schedules. At the same time, Sales Agents receive map-based customer and territory visualization to support canvassing and route planning. Warehouse Personnel can synchronize inventory records across branches, and the Operating Manager and Branch Heads can monitor consolidated operational information through a centralized dashboard that provides insights into collection performance, sales activities, inventory status, customer payment records, branch performance, and operational trends. During requirements-gathering activities, BH Marketing management expressed support for developing the system and recognized its potential to improve operational coordination and decision-making. Given the positive responses from intended users, the modules' alignment with the organization's operational processes, and management's willingness to adopt the system, CORVEX is considered operationally feasible for implementation within BH Marketing.
[Normal] 
[Normal] 2.2.4.2 Technical Feasibility
[Normal] The technical feasibility of CORVEX was evaluated based on the available development resources, existing technical infrastructure, and the system requirements identified through interviews, survey questionnaires, and direct observations conducted at Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing. The system will be developed using React.js for the responsive web interface, Node.js and Express.js for the application server and RESTful API, Python for implementing the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method used in customer prioritization, and PostgreSQL with PostGIS for centralized database and spatial data management. [Normal] 2.3.2  Schedule and Route Generation Using the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) Model 
[Normal] The primary capstone element of CORVEX: A Mapping-Integrated Field Operations and Inventory Centralization System for Multi-Branch Enterprise is implementing the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method as the decision-support mechanism for customer prioritization during collection operations. The SAW method is a Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) technique that evaluates multiple alternatives by normalizing criterion values, applying predefined weights, and computing a weighted preference score to produce a ranked list of alternatives. Because of its computational simplicity, transparency, and effectiveness in evaluating multiple operational criteria simultaneously, SAW has been widely applied in decision-support systems involving resource allocation, scheduling, customer prioritization, and operational planning [19], [20]. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Unlike conventional decision support systems, where the algorithm operates independently, this implementation integrates descriptive analytics with the SAW method. Before ranking, CORVEX first summarizes operational information retrieved from the centralized database, including customer payment records, collection history, and geographic information. These descriptive indicators provide management with an overview of each customer’s financial obligation, payment urgency, and travel accessibility. The summarized operational data then become input variables for the SAW algorithm, which generates an objective customer priority ranking for daily collection activities. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] This implementation directly addresses the operational problems identified during the requirements gathering conducted at Bethuel-Herminihildo (BH) Marketing. Based on interviews with the Operating Manager and field personnel, collectors currently determine collection priorities using personal judgment, considering outstanding balances, overdue payments, and travel distance without any standardized decision-support mechanism. As a result, different collectors may produce different collection schedules for similar customer conditions, leading to inconsistent prioritization, inefficient travel, and reduced collection productivity. Through descriptive analytics and the SAW method, CORVEX standardizes this decision-making process by automatically generating a ranked customer list based on operational data retrieved from the centralized information system. The generated ranking is subsequently displayed in the Collector’s Field Portal to support daily collection planning.
[Normal]

[Normal] The Categories table stores the list of product categories, such as Beverages, Snacks, and Household, referenced by the Products table. It allows inventory and sales records to be grouped and filtered by category for reporting purposes. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 23. Suppliers Table
[Normal] The Suppliers table stores the master information of suppliers that provide products for BH Marketing's inventory replenishment. Restock transactions reference supplier records to identify the source of incoming inventory and support supplier traceability. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 24. Territories Table
[Normal] The territories table stores the service territories assigned to branches and field personnel. It supports map-based visualization, customer assignment, territory coordination, and route planning for field activities. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 25. Users Table
[Normal] The users table stores the accounts of authorized personnel who access CORVEX. Each user is associated with a system role and, where applicable, a branch, allowing the system to enforce role-based and branch-level access to information and functions.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 26. Roles Table
[Normal] The Roles table stores the user roles defined within CORVEX, such as Operating Manager, Branch Head, Collector, Sales Agent, and Warehouse Personnel, along with a display name and a machine-readable slug. The Users and Role Permissions tables reference it to enforce role-based access restrictions. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 27. Permissions Table
[Normal] The Permissions table stores the individual, granular permissions available within the system, including a display label and a description of what each permission allows. It works together with the Roles and Role Permissions tables to implement role-based access control. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 28. Role Permissions Table
[Normal] The Role Permissions table maps roles to their assigned permissions, with a granted flag indicating whether a specific permission is currently active or revoked for that role. It forms the operational core of the system's role-based access control. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 29. Sales Invoices Table
[Normal] The Sales Invoices table stores the header-level information of each sales transaction recorded by a Sales Agent, including the customer, branch, sales agent, total amount, payment method, status, and invoice dates. It serves as the parent record for the Sales Invoice Items table, which stores the individual products included in each sale.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 30. Sales Invoice Items Table
[Normal] The Sales Invoice Items table stores the individual products included in each sales invoice, including the product, quantity, unit price, and line total. It provides the item-level details needed for product-based sales reporting and links to the Sales Invoices table through the invoice identifier.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 31. Field Visits Table
[Normal] The field visit table stores scheduled customer visits assigned to Collectors and Sales Agents, including the customer, assigned personnel, visit type, schedule date, and visit status. Collection visits may be generated from SAW-based customer priority results, while sales visits are managed based on assigned field activities and service territories.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 32. Collection Payment Table
[Normal] The Collection Payment table stores the individual payments received from customers, including the amount, payment method, payment date and time, receiving collector branch, and transaction status. It serves as the source record for digital receipt generation and customer payment history. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 33. Digital Receipts Table
[Normal] The digital receipts table stores electronic receipts generated after successful collection transactions. It provides proof of payment in the offline-capable Field Portal and links directly to collection records.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 34. Credit History Table
[Normal] The credit history table stores historical customer credit-related payment events, including the balance before payment, payment amount, remaining balance, payment status, and transaction date. It references related sales and collection records to provide an auditable history of customer credit activity and support authorized payment and credit verification.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 35. Branch Inventory Table
[Normal] The branch inventory table stores the current inventory quantity and status of each product by BH Marketing branch. It links branches and products and provides the inventory records needed for centralized stock monitoring, availability checks, and inter-branch inventory coordination.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 36. Stock Movements Table
[Normal] The Stock Movements table logs every change in product stock, whether from a sale deduction, restock, inter-branch transfer, adjustment, or damage write-off, along with who performed the movement and when. It provides the audit trail that supports real-time Inventory Centralization across all branches.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 37. Inventory Transfers Table
[Normal] The Inventory Transfers table records requests and completed product movements between BH Marketing branches. It identifies the product, quantity, source branch, destination branch, requesting and approving users, transfer status, and relevant dates to support controlled inter-branch inventory coordination. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 38. Restocks Table
[Normal] The Restocks table records supplier deliveries received by a branch, including the product, quantity, supplier, delivery reference number, and date received. These records update branch inventory levels once new stock arrives, supporting the restocking side of Inventory Centralization. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 39. SAW Results Table
[Normal] The SAW results table stores the calculated preference score and priority rank generated by the SAW collection-prioritization process. These results generate the prioritized collection list for Collectors and preserve the decision-support results for reference and auditing.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 40. Notifications Table
[Normal] The notifications table stores system-generated alerts for collection, inventory, sales scheduling, credit verification, transfer, and synchronization events. These notifications help users stay informed about pending tasks and important system activities.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 41. Field Activity Reports Table
[Normal] The Field Activity Report table stores the field activity reports submitted by Collectors and Sales Agents after a visit, including the activity type, remarks, and optional photographic evidence of the recorded field activity. Its sync_status field supports the offline-capable Field Portal by tracking whether a report has successfully reached the central server. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Table 42. Offline Sync Queue Table
[Normal] The Offline Sync Queue table temporarily stores field records created while connectivity is unavailable and tracks their synchronization state. Once connectivity is restored, the system submits queued records to the application server for validation and synchronization with the centralized PostgreSQL database.

[Normal] 2.3.7 Architecture Design
[Normal] The architectural design of CORVEX illustrates how client-side applications, server-side components, external services, and a centralized database work together to support field operations, Inventory Centralization, and decision support for BH Marketing’s multi-branch operations. The system adopts a three-tier architecture consisting of the Presentation Layer, Application Layer, and Database Layer to ensure scalability, centralized data management, and secure communication between users and enterprise resources. Furthermore, the architecture supports both online and offline field operations, enabling sales agents and collectors to continue recording transactions even when internet connectivity is temporarily unavailable.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Figure 7. CORVEX System Architecture
[Normal] 
[Normal] 2.3.7.1. Presentation Layer 
[Normal] The presentation layer serves as the primary interface through which users interact with CORVEX. It consists of two client applications: the React.js Web Dashboard for administrators, operations managers, and warehouse staff, and the React.js Mobile Field Portal for sales agents and collectors. The web dashboard provides modules for inventory management, operational dashboards, and data visualization. At the same time, the mobile field portal enables collection recording, sales reporting, and interactive map visualization using Leaflet.js integrated with OpenStreetMap, and offline synchronization. User requests are transmitted securely to the server through RESTful web services whenever an internet connection is available.
[Normal] 
[Normal] 2.3.7.2. Application Layer (Server-Side)
[Normal] The application layer contains the core business logic that processes all requests received from the client applications. It manages user authentication and authorization, user management, collection processing, sales management, notification services, analytics, and audit logging. This layer also integrates the SAW Decision Support Engine, which automatically evaluates and ranks customer accounts and sales clients using predefined operational criteria, allowing collectors and sales agents to receive objectives and prioritized daily schedules instead of relying on manual decision-making. Additionally, the application integrates Leaflet.js with OpenStreetMap to provide interactive map visualization of customer locations, territories, and field routes.
[Normal] 
[Normal] 2.3.7.3. Database Layer
[Normal] The database layer utilizes PostgreSQL as the centralized relational database management system that stores all enterprise information. It maintains records for users, branches, clients, products, branch inventory, inventory transfers, sales transactions, collection transactions, credit history, field visits, SAW computation results, notifications, audit logs, and performance summaries. By maintaining a centralized repository, the system ensures operational data from all branches remains synchronized and immediately available for reporting, monitoring, and decision support. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] 2.3.7.4. External Services
[Normal] CORVEX integrates external technologies to enhance its operational capabilities. Leaflet.js and OpenStreetMap provide interactive geographic visualization for customer locations, branch locations, territories, and field routes, supporting location-based operational planning. The system also uses browser-based local storage in the mobile field portal to temporarily store collection and sales transactions. Offline mode allows uninterrupted field operations until synchronization with the central server is possible. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] 2.3.7.5. Online Configuration 
[Normal] During online operation, all user requests submitted through the web dashboard and mobile field portal are transmitted to the application server via secure RESTful communication. The application server validates each request, executes the necessary business logic—including SAW prioritization, Inventory Centralization, mapping integrated processing, and notification generation—and stores the resulting information within the centralized PostgreSQL database. The system then returns updated data to client applications in real time, allowing managers and field personnel across all branches to access consistent, synchronized operational information. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] 2.3.7.6. Offline Configuration
[Normal] When internet connectivity is unavailable, the mobile field portal allows collectors and sales agents to continue recording collections, sales activities, customer visits, and digital receipts using the offline synchronization module. These records are temporarily stored within the device's local storage without interrupting field operations. Once network connectivity is restored, the synchronization module automatically uploads the locally stored transactions to the application server, which validates the data and updates the PostgreSQL database to ensure consistency across all branches.

[Normal] Figure 8. Collector's Today's Route Screen 
[Normal] 
[Normal] The Today's Route screen displays the daily prioritized collection list generated by the Collection Dispatch Engine using the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method, ranking customer accounts by outstanding balance, days since last visit, and geographic distance. Collectors use this screen at the start of each field day to view assigned stops, identify overdue accounts, and check planned travel distance before deployment. When a collector selects "View" for an account, the system should display detailed customer and balance information. At the same time, the Map View and Route Summary tabs allow the collector to visualize the same prioritized route geographically and review aggregate route statistics. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Figure 9. Sales Agent's Today's Schedule Screen 
[Normal] 
[Normal] The Today's Schedule screen displays the prioritized client visit list generated by the Sales Scheduling Engine using the Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) method, ranking sales clients by purchase history volume, days since last sales visit, and geographic distance. Sales Agents use this screen at the start of each field day to view their ranked client visits, track visit status (Pending, Completed, Rescheduled), and check total distance and stops for the day. From this screen, the agent is expected to view client details, log a visit, call the client, or open the client's location on the map, with each completed action updating the visit status in real time. 
[Normal] 
[Normal] Figure 10. Collector's Field Portal Dashboard  
[Normal] The Field Portal Dashboard serves as the Collector's home screen, summarizing the day's workload through key metrics, accounts assigned, amounts collected, pending visits, and completed visits, along with recent collection activity and incident reports. Collectors use this screen at the start of their field day and throughout to get an at-a-glance view of progress and launch core Field Portal actions such as starting today's route, reporting an incident, or reviewing assigned accounts. The system is expected to update all summary figures and the route progress bar in real time as the collector logs collections and completes visits through the offline-capable Field Portal.

[Normal] Figure 11. Collector's Collection Log Screen 
[Normal] This form lets the collector record a payment received from a client during a field visit. The collector enters the amount collected, selects the payment method (cash, check, bank transfer, mobile), uploads a photo of the proof of payment, and optionally marks it as a partial payment. On submission, the system validates the amount against the outstanding balance, logs the transaction, generates a digital receipt, and deducts the collected amount from the client's outstanding balance in real time.
[Normal] 
[Normal] Figure 12. Warehouse Staff's Inventory Screen 
[Normal] This is the warehouse personnel's primary working screen, showing all furniture products assigned to their branch with current stock levels, reorder thresholds, and status badges (Sufficient, Low Stock, Critical, Out of Stock). Staff use it to monitor which items need restocking or transferring and to perform stock counts. The system automatically flags any item at or below its reorder point with a warning, and items at zero stock trigger a critical alert that propagates up to the Branch Manager and Operating Manager.
